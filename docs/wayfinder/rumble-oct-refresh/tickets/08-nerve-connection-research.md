## Question

What does high-quality evidence say about one spinal cause (cervical/thoracic cord compression, myelopathy, nerve-root compression) explaining together: hyperreflexia, sciatica, and cold-triggered white numb index fingertips? What are the competing explanations (Raynaud's, peripheral neuropathy, thoracic outlet, carpal tunnel)? What tests would distinguish them (MRI levels, Hoffmann sign, NCS/EMG, cold-provocation)? Output feeds the Nerves encyclopaedia outline and clinician-question list. Decision support only; save to `agent_reports/`.


## Resolution

No single spinal lesion plausibly explains all three symptoms. Hyperreflexia fits cervical (or thoracic) cord dysfunction, consistent with the post-C5-C7-fusion UMN concern; sciatica fits lumbar root compression (which lowers, not raises, reflexes); cold-triggered white fingertips are the classic picture of Raynaud phenomenon (vascular), not cord/root compression. Tandem cervical-lumbar stenosis is a recognised overlap. Distinguishing tests: full UMN exam incl. Hoffmann, cervical MRI (adjacent levels C4-C5, C7-T1, cord signal), thoracic/lumbar MRI as indicated, NCS/EMG, capillaroscopy plus ANA/ENA bloods, positional duplex for TOS.
