## 1. Urgent Clinical Addendum: Rapidly Deteriorating Gait & High-Steppage Deficit

### Patient-Reported Symptom
> *"Inability to walk or step high, I trip over things daily and it's deteriorating rapidly."*

### Pathophysiological Breakdown (MDT Synthesis: Neurosurgeon + Pain Specialist)

#### Differential Diagnosis 1: Acute L4/L5 Radicular Motor Deficit (Foot Drop / Steppage Gait)
* **Mechanics:** Inability to clear the ground without lifting the knee excessively ("high steppage gait") and catching the toes/tripping is the hallmark of **ankle dorsiflexion weakness (Foot Drop)**.
* **Innervation:**
  - **L4 Nerve Root:** Tibialis anterior (foot inversion and primary dorsiflexion).
  - **L5 Nerve Root:** Extensor hallucis longus (great toe dorsiflexion) and extensor digitorum longus.
* **Correlation:** Directly correlates with the severe 24/7 right lumbar pain and provocative dural shockwaves on slight forward flexion. An enlarging focal disc protrusion/extrusion at L4–L5 or L5–S1 can compress the motor root, causing rapid loss of motor power.

#### Differential Diagnosis 2: Cervical/Thoracic Myelopathy Progression (Upper Motor Neuron Spastic Gait)
* **Mechanics:** The patient has a verified history of severe cervical cord myelopathy and residual cord damage (myelomalacia) prior to the June 2026 fusion.
* **Manifestation:** Loss of proprioception, leg stiffness, impaired foot clearance, and hyperreflexia/clonus can cause sudden gait ataxia, tripping, and loss of motor control.
* **Red Flag Status:** Rapid deterioration over days to weeks is a critical neurological red flag requiring immediate differentiation between peripheral root compression (LMN) and central cord compression (UMN).
