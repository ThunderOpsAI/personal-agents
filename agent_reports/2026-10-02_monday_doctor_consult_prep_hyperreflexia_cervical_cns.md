## 2. Neuro-Anatomical Pathophysiology: What Is It Called & Why Does It Happen?

### The Clinical Term
The medical term for when the knee kicks out exaggeratedly upon light touch or percussion is **Hyperreflexia** (specifically **Patellar Hyperreflexia**, **Hyperactive Deep Tendon Reflex**, or a **Brisk Knee Jerk**). 

When the reflex triggers repetitive, rhythmic, involuntary muscle beats or shuddering, it is termed **Patellar Clonus**.

### Pathophysiological Mechanism
The patellar reflex is a monosynaptic stretch reflex mediated locally in the lower back at the **L2, L3, and L4 spinal cord segments** via the femoral nerve.

```
  [ Brain / Motor Cortex ]
            │
            ▼ (Descending Corticospinal Tracts - Normally INHIBIT and modulate reflex sensitivity)
  ══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
  [ CERVICAL SPINAL CORD (C5-C7) ] <── Prior Compression / Fusion Site (24 June 2026)
  ══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
            │  If descending inhibitory signals are blocked or slowed:
            ▼
  [ LUMBAR SPINAL CORD (L2-L4) ]
            │  L2-L4 reflex arc is DISINHIBITED (Hyperactive)
            ▼
  [ Femoral Nerve & Quadriceps ] ──> Explosive Patellar Kick / Hyperreflexia (3+ to 4+)
```

* **Upper Motor Neuron (UMN) Sign:** Normal brain and brainstem pathways constantly fire descending inhibitory signals down the spinal cord to "dampen" peripheral reflexes.
* **Loss of Central Inhibition:** When the cervical spinal cord (corticospinal tract) suffers mechanical compression, ischemia, or residual damage (myelomalacia), these inhibitory signals cannot pass effectively. As a result, the lumbar reflex arc is freed from central regulation (disinhibition), firing violently even upon trivial contact.
* **Anatomical Level:** Because the reflex is hyperactive in the *lower* limb, the causative lesion must be located **above** L2—in this case, concordant with the known cervical spinal cord injury.
